1. Student Accommodation Hub

### Problem
Finding housing is difficult and stressful. Listings are scattered across word of mouth, notice boards, social media groups and agents. Students struggle to verify that a room exists, compare prices, or judge safety and distance from campus. New and first-year students are especially exposed to scams and unfair pricing. Landlords also struggle to reach students who are actively looking.

### Proposed Solution
A centralised platform that connects all student housing in one place**: on-campus hostels, off-campus rentals and shared rooms. Students search, filter and compare listings. Landlords and hostel managers publish and manage their properties.

### Target Users
- Students looking for housing (especially first years and transfers)
- Landlords, hostel owners and agents
- University accommodation office (as an optional partner or verifier)

### Key Features
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Unified listings | Rooms and houses with photos, price, rules, amenities and availability |
| Search and filters | Price range, distance to campus, room type, water/electricity, security |
| Map view | See properties relative to campus and transport routes |
| Verified listings | Badge for properties confirmed by a physical check or the university |
| Reviews and ratings | Honest feedback from current and past tenants |
| Roommate matching | Students looking to share can find each other |
| Enquiry and booking requests | In-app messaging and viewing requests |
| Landlord dashboard | Manage listings, vacancies and enquiries |
| Report listing | Flag fake or misleading adverts |

## MVP Scope
Listing creation, search with filters, property detail page, enquiry/contact button, admin approval of listings.

### Risks and Challenges
- Fake or outdated listings, so a verification and expiry process is needed
- Getting enough landlords onboarded at launch (supply side first)
- Landlords with limited internet access, so an assisted onboarding option may be needed

### Success Metrics
- Number of verified listings
- Number of students who found housing through the platform
- Average time to find a room
- Landlord return rate (repeat listings)

## 2. Electronics Repair Marketplace

### Problem
When a phone or laptop breaks, students do not know where to go. Quality is inconsistent, prices are unclear, repairs are sometimes delayed or poorly done, and there is little recourse when something goes wrong. For students, a broken laptop or phone directly affects their studies.

### Proposed Solution
A platform where students can **find and access certified repair shops**, with **live feedback from both sides** (student and shop) throughout the repair, from request to collection.

### Target Users
- Students with faulty phones, laptops and accessories
- Certified repair shops and technicians
- Platform administrators (certification and dispute handling)

### Key Features
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Certified shop directory | Shops are vetted before being listed and shown with a certified badge |
| Service and price listing | Shops list repairs offered with estimated price ranges |
| Repair request | Student describes the fault, uploads photos and chooses a shop |
| Live status tracking | Statuses such as *Received, Diagnosed, Awaiting approval, In repair, Ready for collection* |
| Quote approval | Student approves the quote before work begins |
| Two-way feedback | Student rates the shop, and shop rates the student (e.g. reliability, communication) |
| Live chat | Direct messaging for updates and clarifications |
| Warranty record | Record of the repair and any warranty period offered |
| Dispute handling | Escalation route when a repair goes wrong |

### MVP Scope
Shop profiles and certification flag, repair request form, status updates, chat, and two-way rating after completion.

### Risks and Challenges
- Defining and enforcing "certified": criteria and a review process are needed
- Shop owners may be reluctant to adopt a new tool, so it must be very simple to use
- Review fairness: ratings need moderation to prevent abuse

### Success Metrics
- Number of certified shops onboarded
- Number of completed repairs
- Average rating from both sides
- Dispute rate and resolution time

## 3. Student Transport Connect

### Problem
Students often lack a reliable way to get between campus, hostels, town and markets, especially early in the morning, late at night or in bad weather. Finding a bike taxi or other transport means asking around, and prices and safety are uncertain.

### Proposed Solution
A platform that **connects students directly to bikers and other transport providers**. Students request a ride, and nearby registered riders and drivers can accept it.

### Target Users
- Students needing transport
- Bike taxi riders and other registered transport providers
- Campus administration or security (optional partner for rider vetting)

### Key Features
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Ride request | Choose pickup and destination (campus points, hostels, town) |
| Rider matching | Request goes to nearby available riders |
| Registered riders | Rider profiles with photo, vehicle details and ID verification |
| Fare guidance | Standard fares for common routes to avoid overcharging |
| Ratings | Students rate riders and riders rate students |
| Safety tools | Share trip details with a friend, emergency contact button |
| Scheduled rides | Book ahead for early lectures or exams |
| Multiple transport types | Bike taxis, minibuses, shared rides, with room to grow |
| Rider dashboard | Earnings summary and trip history |

### MVP Scope
Rider registration and verification, ride request and accept flow, fixed route fare table, ratings.

### Risks and Challenges
- Rider safety and trust: verification is essential
- Cold start: enough riders must be available for students to rely on the service
- Connectivity: an SMS or USSD fallback could help where data is limited
- Payments: cash first, with mobile money integration later

### Success Metrics
- Number of verified riders
- Rides completed per week
- Average pickup wait time
- Safety incidents (target: zero) and average ratings

## 4. Anonymous Mental Health Support

### Problem
Students face academic pressure, financial stress, homesickness and other challenges, but many do not seek help because of stigma, fear of judgment, or not knowing who to approach. Counselling services may exist but can feel intimidating or hard to reach.

### Proposed Solution
A platform that gives students **anonymous access to a helper within the school**, such as a counsellor, trained peer supporter or student-welfare officer. Students can reach out safely without revealing their identity.

### Target Users
- Students seeking support
- Counsellors, trained peer supporters and welfare staff
- University student affairs and health services

### Key Features
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Anonymous access | No real name required; random alias or ID per user |
| Private chat | Text-based conversation with an available helper |
| Helper matching | Choose a topic (stress, relationships, finances, academics) and get routed |
| Booking an in-person session | Optional step to move from anonymous chat to a face-to-face appointment, only if the student chooses |
| Self-help resources | Articles, breathing exercises, study-stress tips |
| Crisis protocol | Clear escalation path and emergency contacts if a student may be at risk |
| Helper training and guidelines | Confidentiality rules, boundaries and referral practices |
| Feedback | Anonymous satisfaction feedback on sessions |

### MVP Scope
Anonymous sign-in with alias, one-to-one chat with a helper, topic selection, resource library, and a visible crisis-support page.

### Risks and Challenges
- **Privacy and confidentiality are the core of this product.** Data must be minimal, encrypted and never linked to student identity
- Helpers must be properly trained, and the platform must not replace professional care
- A clear crisis escalation process must be agreed with the university *before* launch
- Abuse prevention, so that the service is not misused, while keeping anonymity

### Success Metrics
- Number of students reached (anonymous counts only)
- Helper response time
- Share of conversations that lead to appropriate referral or follow-up
- Anonymous satisfaction scores

## Shared Design Principles

These apply to whichever idea is taken forward:

- **Mobile first.** Most students rely on smartphones.
- **Low-data friendly.** Lightweight pages and the option of SMS/USSD or WhatsApp-style flows.
- **Mobile money ready.** Airtel Money and TNM Mpamba integration where payments are involved.
- **Trust by design.** Verification, ratings and clear reporting tools.
- **Simple for providers.** Landlords, shop owners and riders must be able to use it with minimal training.
- **Privacy respected.** Collect only the data that is needed.

## Suggested Technical Direction

Not final, to be decided by the team that builds each idea:

- **Frontend:** React / React Native or Flutter (cross-platform mobile)
- **Backend:** Node.js (Express) or Django
- **Database:** PostgreSQL or Firebase
- **Maps and location:** OpenStreetMap or Google Maps
- **Notifications:** SMS gateway and push notifications
- **Hosting:** Any affordable cloud provider

## Suggested Next Steps

1. **Validate:** Interview 10 to 20 students per idea to confirm the problem and willingness to use a solution.
2. **Prioritise:** Score each idea on impact, feasibility and team skills, then choose one to build first.
3. **Define the MVP:** Lock the smallest feature set that proves value.
4. **Prototype:** Create wireframes and a clickable demo.
5. **Pilot:** Launch with a small group on campus and gather feedback.
6. **Partner:** Engage the university (accommodation office, student affairs, counselling services) early.
